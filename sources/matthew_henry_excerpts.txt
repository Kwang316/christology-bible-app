Matthew Henry — Commentary on the Whole Bible (1706)
Selected Excerpts — Public Domain

---

On the Nature of Numbers and Divine Order (Numbers 1)

God commanded Moses to number the people of Israel. This numbering was not a mere act of curiosity or political census, but a declaration of God's care and knowledge of every individual soul. The God who numbers the hairs of our heads also numbers His people. Every soul that belongs to the Lord is precious and accounted for. To number is to value; to count is to cherish.

The measure and the number belong to God: "He telleth the number of the stars; he calleth them all by their names" (Psalm 147:4). Creation itself is a work of divine mathematics — each star named, each sparrow noted, each human being fearfully and wonderfully made. The infinite God takes count of the finite creation.

On Wisdom and Understanding (Proverbs 1–3)

The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom — this is the foundation of all true knowledge. What the world calls wisdom apart from God is foolishness. All learning, whether of numbers, of nature, or of human affairs, finds its proper center when it flows back to its Creator. The wise man does not merely accumulate knowledge; he applies it in humility and reverence.

Those who measure and count and calculate without acknowledging the God who formed the capacity for measurement are like a man who uses a gift without thanking the giver. All human faculties — reason, memory, the ability to count — are gifts from the Creator.

On Love (1 Corinthians 13)

Charity, which is love, is described by the Apostle Paul as the greatest of all Christian graces. Without love, all gifts are empty — even the gift of prophecy, of knowledge, of faith great enough to remove mountains. Love is patient and kind; it does not envy or boast. It bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things.

The love of God is not a sentiment but an action. God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son. Christian love is therefore not merely an emotion to be felt but a sacrifice to be made. We love because He first loved us.

On Prayer (Matthew 6)

Our Lord teaches us to pray with simplicity and sincerity. Prayer is not a performance before men but a conversation with the Father who sees in secret. The model prayer given by Christ — what we call the Lord's Prayer — covers every need of the believing soul: adoration of God's name, submission to His kingdom and will, petition for daily sustenance, forgiveness of sins, and deliverance from evil.

Prayer is the breath of the soul. As the body cannot live without breathing, so the spiritual life cannot be maintained without prayer. He who neglects prayer neglects the greatest privilege granted to mortal man.

On Faith (Hebrews 11)

Faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen. This definition by the Apostle Paul shows that faith is not blind credulity but a well-grounded confidence in the word and character of God. Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him for righteousness. The heroes of faith listed in this chapter — from Abel to the prophets — all acted on the unseen promises of God, often at great cost to themselves.

Faith does not require that all questions be answered before trust is given. Rather, faith trusts the character of the One who has spoken, knowing that He who promised is faithful.

On Salvation (John 3)

Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews, came to Jesus by night. The conversation that followed produced the most celebrated verse in all of Scripture: "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life." Here is the Gospel in miniature — the love of God, the gift of His Son, the condition of faith, the promise of eternal life.

Salvation is entirely of grace, not of works. No man can earn or deserve it. It is the free gift of a merciful God to guilty sinners. The new birth that Christ spoke of to Nicodemus is a sovereign work of the Holy Spirit, without which no man can see the kingdom of God.
