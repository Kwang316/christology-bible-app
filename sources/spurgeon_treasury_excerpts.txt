On Psalm 19 — The Heavens Declare the Glory of God

"The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork." (Psalm 19:1)

What a text is here! The vault of heaven is God's cathedral, the sun His candle, the stars His jewels. Every morning the sun rises as a bridegroom coming out of his chamber, and there is nothing hidden from the heat of it. The universe is a sermon without words, a proof without argument, a revelation without a prophet. Day after day it speaks; night after night it shows knowledge.

The measure of the heavens — their distance, their number, their order — declares a God of precision and design. The astronomer who measures the heavens is tracing the handiwork of God. All science, rightly done, is an act of worship.

On Psalm 23 — The Lord is My Shepherd

"The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want." (Psalm 23:1)

What a shepherd! Not a hireling who flees at the approach of the wolf, but the Good Shepherd who lays down his life for the sheep. The believer is a sheep — helpless, prone to wander, easily led astray — but the Shepherd is omniscient, omnipresent, and omnipotent. He knows His sheep by name; He counts them; He leads them in paths of righteousness.

"I shall not want" — what a promise! Not "I may not want" or "I hope not to want" but "I shall not want." The Lord provides, protects, restores, and finally brings His sheep safely home. The green pastures and still waters are not merely poetic images but the daily experience of the trusting soul.

On Psalm 46 — God is Our Refuge and Strength

"God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble." (Psalm 46:1)

Trouble will come. The psalmist does not pretend otherwise. The earth may remove, the mountains may be carried into the midst of the sea, the waters may roar and foam — but the believer has a refuge. God Himself is the hiding place of His people. Not a distant fortress to be reached by long journey, but a "very present" help — near, available, sufficient.

Strength for service and courage in suffering both flow from this same source. The man who knows God as his refuge does not fear though the earth be removed, for his foundation is not in the earth but in the eternal God.

On Psalm 119 — The Word of God

"Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path." (Psalm 119:105)

The Word of God is the Christian's guide in a dark world. Note the precision: "a lamp unto my feet" — not a floodlight illuminating the distant horizon, but sufficient light for the next step. God rarely shows us the entire journey; He gives us light enough for obedience today. The path of the just is as the shining light, shining more and more unto the perfect day.

The Psalmist loved the law of God and meditated on it day and night. This love for Scripture is the mark of a regenerate soul. Cold, formal reading of the Bible profits little; the soul must feed upon it as the body feeds upon bread — with appetite, with relish, with thanksgiving.
